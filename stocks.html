New to stocks? Start here — plain-English basics (click to collapse)

A stock is a tiny slice of ownership in a company. When you own a share of Apple stock, you own a small piece of Apple. Its price goes up when more people want to buy than sell, and down when more people want to sell than buy.

A ticker symbol is a company's short nickname on the stock market — e.g. AAPL = Apple, TSLA = Tesla, SPY/QQQ = popular index funds (see the Index Funds page for what those track).

  • This page only tracks prices — it doesn't buy, sell, or connect to a real brokerage account. To actually invest, you'd open an account at a real brokerage (see the Index Funds & Brokerages page) and place the trade there.
  • Your watchlist is just the list of tickers you're keeping an eye on — add or remove any with the box below the chart.
  • Every column has its own "?" button — click any of them for a plain-English explanation.

Select a stock to view its price history This line traces the stock's closing price each trading day. Sloping up means the price rose over that period; sloping down means it fell. It shows the past only — it can't predict what happens next.

Changes how far back the chart looks: 1M = past 1 month, 3M = past 3 months, and so on. Zooming out (6M/1Y) shows the bigger long-term trend; zooming in (1M) shows recent, day-to-day movement.
